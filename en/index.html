QUEST 01 PERSONAL PROJECT 2026

This website

PROBLEM

Developer websites all look the same: same template, same animations, same sentences. None of them prove anything — they describe skills instead of showing them. I wanted a site that was the evidence, not a description of the evidence.

APPROACH

No framework and no component library. I wrote a pixel-art sprite engine from scratch: every icon is a 16×16 grid drawn by hand on a 16-colour palette — exactly the constraint of a SNES tile — rendered to canvas and animated with palette cycling.

The 3D uses three.js but renders at a fifth of the real resolution with antialiasing off, and lets CSS scale it back up: the polygons pick up the same hard edges as the sprites, the way they did on 16-bit hardware. Both scenes only run while they are on screen.

Accessibility was verified by measurement, not by eye: a script walks every text element in both themes and computes its contrast ratio against the real background. It caught three bugs that were invisible to the eye, including an unreadable button in the mobile menu.

RESULT

A page that explains itself to anyone who can read the source, and that the people who cannot still remember. No build step anywhere.

FREQUENTLY ASKED

The things people always ask

How long have you been doing this?

I have been writing code since I was sixteen and I do it for a living: I build software full time for a large company.

17Labs is the name I take my own projects under, and it has just started. So what you will find below is documented personal work rather than a client list — that is a choice, not a gap papered over with borrowed names.

What does it cost, and how long does it take?

It genuinely depends on the project, and I will spare you the invented price list: a marketing site and a system covering five departments have nothing in common, in either price or time.

The fastest way to find out is a call. I will ask a few questions about what happens in your process today and give you an honest estimate — and if the job does not make sense, I will tell you instead of selling it to you.

How many projects do you run at once?

One. When I take a job I am fully in it, with no queue and no splitting myself between clients competing for the same hours.

That is the concrete advantage of hiring one person instead of a studio: you are never the project that waits.

What happens after delivery?

Delivery includes a support period. It is deliberately short — roughly a month — because its job is to cover the things that only surface once the software is genuinely in use: a fix, a detail to adjust, a question from someone using it every day.

If you want to continue after that, it extends. But I would rather it were your choice once you have seen how it goes, than a signature collected beforehand.

Do you work remotely?

Yes, anywhere. I work fully remotely with no geographic limits: what it takes is a video call now and then and a written channel where you can reach me.

I work in English and Italian.

Who owns the code you write?

The project code is yours. What I write for you — your process logic, your data model, your interfaces — is handed over with full repository access and stays yours. If you ever want to change supplier or bring the work in house, you do not need my permission.

There is one exception, and it is only fair to state it up front: the generic libraries and components I carry from one project to the next stay mine, and you get an unlimited, perpetual licence to use them within your project. That is not there to lock you in — it is what lets me avoid starting from zero every time, which is what makes the work cost you less.

The only thing I keep is the right to write the project up in my portfolio. If you would rather I did not, just say so.

Can you take over a project someone else started?

It depends, and it needs looking at before anyone promises anything. Send me the code and tell me where things stopped: I will assess how much is salvageable, what is worth rewriting, and whether it makes more sense to continue or restart.

You get that assessment either way, even if we then decide not to work together. A clear answer beats a quote for something I have not seen.
